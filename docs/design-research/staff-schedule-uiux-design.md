# /staff/schedule — the day sheet, designed

**Status:** design brief · **owner rulings on Q2 (auto-confirm) and Q3 (staff may move) granted 2026-08-07**; Q1 and Q4–Q7 still open (§11)
**Scope:** the `/staff/schedule` surface only — its representation, its anatomy, its verbs, and the ranked feature set
**Companion:** [staff-surfaces-proposal.md](./staff-surfaces-proposal.md) — this document refines §4 and shares its phase letters (§10)
**Date:** 2026-08-07

---

## 1. The one-paragraph shape

`/staff/schedule` is **the complete run**: every rostered minute of every working chair is accounted for by exactly one row, and nothing else is on screen. A visit is a row. The hole between two visits is a row. A break, a sick day, an admin block is a row. A barber who is not working today has no lane at all. That single idea collapses the two questions the shop actually asks — _who is booked_ and _what can I sell_ — into one read, because the absence of a segment fill **is** the gap, and it removes the surface's largest current lie, which is that a barber on holiday and a barber with a wide-open Saturday both render as "Free day". The run is a semantic `ul`/`li` list at every width; on a laptop the same `li` elements gain proportional height against one shared time axis through a container query, so a laptop is a zoom level of the phone rather than a second product. Today's surface is a sparse list of bookings with a wrapping button cluster under each one; the work is to make the run complete, to make the row one scannable line with one verb, and to stop the screen from stating things that are not true.

## 2. The central decision: one complete run, gaining a second axis — not a grid, not a mode switch

This is the section the rest of the document hangs off, and the research disagreed sharply. Three positions were argued:

| Position                                                                                                                          | Argued by                                                                                 | Cost                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A.** Proportional resource-column time grid as the primary surface, agenda list as a segmented alternate rendering              | interaction lens, feasibility lens                                                        | New layout primitive; drags dead-time compression, a 44pt-vs-true-height trade, party connectors, and a mode-default heuristic behind it |
| **B.** Keep the list; add roster-driven lanes and gap rows                                                                        | competitive lens, product critic                                                          | One extra injected port, zero new primitives                                                                                             |
| **C.** One DOM — semantic `ul[uiListGroup]`/`li[uiListRow]` — with CSS Grid placing the same `li` proportionally on the wide tier | accessibility lens (filed under a11y, where nobody making layout decisions would read it) | CSS plus a container query                                                                                                               |

> ### ⚠️ OVERRULED BY THE OWNER, 2026-08-07 — this section is kept for its reasoning, not its conclusion
>
> The shop asked for **four views behind a toolbar switch**: list, day, 3-day and week, drawn as calendars in the manner of Apple Calendar. That reverses the ruling below _and_ the week/3-day rejections in §8. It shipped the same day.
>
> **What survived the reversal, because it was arithmetic rather than taste:** five chairs across seven days is thirty-five columns, and no amount of design solves that. So the views split on what a COLUMN is —
>
> | View                 | A column is                | Barber scope                                                         |
> | -------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
> | **List**             | — (the complete run of §4) | all barbers                                                          |
> | **Day**              | a **barber**               | all barbers                                                          |
> | **3-day** / **Week** | a **date**                 | a barber picker, defaulting to all-merged with per-block attribution |
>
> **The picker is a PORTRAIT, not a labelled button** — the shop knows its own barbers by face, and "everyone" gets the `groups` glyph on an avatar-sized disc, the identical treatment the booking flow gives _Anyone_. The two surfaces say the same thing the same way. The **view** switcher is an icon-only pull-down whose glyph is the current view; the labels stay inside the menu, which is the only place that teaches which glyph means what.
>
> ⚠️ An earlier pass of this section called these "chairs". That was invented here — the domain type is `Barber`, the booking flow says barbers, and a picker of people should name people.
>
> Two consequences worth writing down. **Gaps are omitted once chairs are merged into a date column** — Ivan's free 14:00 is not the shop's free 14:00, and drawing both as one hole in one column would state something untrue. And the grid's vertical axis is **minutes from midnight**, not epoch instants, because Monday's 09:00 and Tuesday's 09:00 are twenty-four hours apart in absolute time; that normalisation is also what makes a 23- or 25-hour DST day need no special case.
>
> One renderer serves all three ([staff-time-grid.ts](../../libs/features/staff/dashboard/src/lib/time-grid/staff-time-grid.ts)); the views differ only in the columns handed to it. The vertical extent comes from the **roster**, which is what §2 always claimed it should — so an empty week still draws its days instead of collapsing to nothing.

**Ruling: C, with B's data model, and A rejected as a mode.** The list and the grid are not rivals and they must not be a switch. `ui-list-group`'s selector already accepts `ul[uiListGroup]` and `ui-list-row` already accepts `li[uiListRow]` — [list-row.ts:75](../../libs/ui/patterns/src/lib/list-row/list-row.ts:75) — with the comment naming schedules as the motivating case. On the wide tier those same `li` elements take `grid-row` from a computed minute track. There is no second DOM, no `role="grid"`, no `aria-hidden` twin to drift, and nothing exists purely for layout.

Three reasons this beats A:

1. **A mode switch is an admission that the surface has two organizing ideas.** A distracted person opening this screen must find it looking the way it looked yesterday. Apple's calendars switch _range_ (day/week/month), never _canvas_. The repo's own availability ruling (§9e of [availability-and-statistics-design.md](./availability-and-statistics-design.md)) says map and list are "not modes, they are the same question at two zoom levels" — the interaction lens cited that in support of a mode switch, and it is the argument against one.
2. **The grid's payoff is the complete run, not the pixels.** What A buys is that a gap is visible and absence differs from free. A gap row inside the existing `ui-list-group` delivers both, against markup that already exists, with one extra injected port. Proportional height is the _refinement_, and it is worth having on a laptop where four columns must align at 15:00 — which is the only thing that justifies drawing lanes side by side at all.
3. **A drags a tail nobody costed.** Shop-wide dead-time compression exists only because proportional pixels create acreage; once a three-hour hole costs 44pt instead of 400pt, compression is a solution to a problem we do not have.

**The two form factors are one design.** Below 760px: one lane at a time, vertical, thumb-reachable, rows at natural height. At 1024px and up: lanes side by side in a `ui-grid`, one time gutter reading down the leading edge, rows proportional, and `data-density="compact"` on the schedule region. That density attribute is already a wired app-level axis — [tokens.css:223](../../libs/ui/tokens/tokens.css:223) — and not one of a hundred research findings reached for it; the interaction lens proposed hand-authoring a denser row variant instead. Every spacing token derives from `--sys-space-unit`, so one declaration steps gaps, control heights and gutters down together, which is what makes density read as calm rather than cramped.

**Ruling: the laptop buys a second axis and a density step. It never buys a different set of controls.** A receptionist who learns the row at the desk and then picks up a phone must find the same row. This forecloses "three verbs inline on the laptop, one plus overflow on the phone."

**Ruling: cap the visible lanes at four; the rest reach through the working/everyone toggle.** Five chairs × seven days is thirty-five columns; there is no week grid here, ever (§8).

**One stated trade, written down now rather than discovered later.** On the proportional tier a 15-minute service is roughly 30pt tall at any legible scale — below the 44 rung. A row never renders shorter than 44pt; below that threshold the label degrades in a fixed order (time + name → name → initials) and the true duration draws as an inset accent rule inside the 44pt box. **The tap target may lie about the height by a few points. The schedule never lies about the time.**

## 4. The anatomy of the screen

### 4.1 The header

The title `h1` reading "Schedule" and a date the user already knows currently spend roughly 120pt — a third of a phone fold — on zero operational content. **Ruling: collapse the `h1` into the toolbar using the house `uiSheetLargeTitle` grammar. The date becomes the control.**

- Tapping the date opens a sheet holding `ui-calendar-scroller` (already built, already used by `/book`), so any day in the horizon is two taps. Chevrons stay for ±1.
- The day lives in the URL: `/staff/schedule?day=2026-08-07`, absent meaning today. A day that cannot be pasted into a staff chat has the same problem the routes file already argues about bookmarks.
- **`todayKey` becomes a signal**, re-read on `visibilitychange` and on a timer to the next Sofia midnight. When it rolls over while the sheet sits on the old day, the header keeps the old date and adds a warning-tone `ui-status-indicator`: _"This is yesterday's schedule."_ **No auto-jump** — moving the day under someone mid-tap is its own trust failure.
- A quiet `ui-status-indicator` appears beside the date **only when the sheet is not live** — "Offline · last updated 14:12". Nothing when live.
- **Past days, and days beyond `horizonMonths`, are distinct states.** Beyond the horizon the lanes do not render "Free day"; the page says _"beyond the booking window."_ This is the same fabricated-availability failure as the holiday case, in a second place nobody checked. `horizonEndFrom(today)` already computes the edge.

### 4.2 NOW and NEXT

Above the run, on **today only**, a two-row `ui-list-group`:

- **NOW** — avatar, client, service, trailing "18 min left".
- **NEXT** — time, client, service, and that row's one primary verb.

Both tap into the visit sheet. Inside the run, the now-line is a 1pt accent hairline with the time as a small accent bead in the gutter; on mount the run scrolls it to one third from the top — more future than past, once, never fighting a manual scroll. One shared minute tick, paused on `document.hidden`; **no ticking signal exists in the repo today** (grep finds no `setInterval` outside specs) and it must be one shared signal, not one per row.

The card degrades honestly: no visit in progress reads "Next in 40 min"; after the last visit, "Nothing more today"; on any day that is not today the card and the hairline **disappear rather than lie**.

**Rejected here:** a ticking `document.title` and Web Notifications. Both put a client's name into the tab strip, the browser history, or the OS notification shade of a device that lives on a shared counter — the exact discipline §9.3 of the proposal applies to absence reasons. The sticky header is the only rung. It is also the one element on this screen that earns a material, under the standing chrome-only ruling.

### 4.3 The row — the biggest craft defect on the surface

The current row stacks **five lines** into default projection: time + badge, service, client + phone, note, then a wrapping button cluster. `ui-list-row` documents a leading / label / trailing anatomy and this template uses **none of the three slots**, while `uiTrailing` is used correctly across a dozen other features. Four separate research findings argued about _which verbs_ the row shows; none asked what the row _is_. At forty rows a five-line record is a wall, and no amount of verb discipline fixes it.

**Ruling: the row is one line.**

| Slot         | Carries                                                                          | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `uiLeading`  | Start time                                                                       | `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` — **absent today**, and the times are the leftmost thing on every row. Non-tabular figures do not align down a column, and the wide tier's cross-lane alignment is invisible without it. |
| label        | **Client name**                                                                  | The thing the eye must land on in under three seconds. Currently third.                                                                                                                                                       |
| `uiTrailing` | Service (secondary ink) · state glyph · note glyph · `tel:` glyph · **one** verb | Hugged trailing.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| row itself   | `data-status`, `data-terminal`, `[data-selected]`                                | Status is word + shape + colour (§9).                                                                                                                                                                                         |

Two rulings inside that:

**Ruling: the phone number lives in the visit sheet, not in the run.** Two lenses independently wanted tap-to-call and both left the digits printed inline. On a busy Saturday that renders forty clients' phone numbers in plain text on a device that faces the room, and it costs a full line of every row. The _action_ is right; the payload is not. The row carries a `tel:` glyph at the 44 rung — **absent, not disabled**, on an anonymous walk-in seat that has no number.

**Ruling: the client note is a glyph on the row and prose only in the sheet.** Same reasoning. The screen is held in front of the person it describes; the moment a client reads what the shop wrote about them, the shop stops writing honestly and the client record dies.

**Densities.** Phone: one line, verb visible. Laptop (`data-density="compact"`, proportional): same line; below 44pt of true duration the label degrades time+name → name → initials.

### 4.4 The gap row, and the three kinds of nothing

A gap is a `ui-list-row` **with no segment fill** inside a run of filled ones. No dashes, no hatching, no new texture — the absence of the fill _is_ the gap, and this system has no vocabulary for either.

- Label: `13:20 → 14:00 · 40 min free`.
- Filtered by **the shop's actual shortest sellable service**, never `foldBarberDay`'s 15-minute default, which the code itself describes as deliberately small for reporting. Set that bar low and the lane becomes a ladder of alternating booking/gap/booking, and the three-second read is gone.
- A gap inside `minLeadMinutes` or already past renders quieter and carries "outside the booking window" when opened — staff may place there, the public may not ([roster-window.ts](../../libs/domain/scheduling/src/lib/roster-window.ts), owner ruling R1).
- A gap with waitlist demand carries a count badge and, if the matcher has already fired, its fanout state: _"offered to 2 at 11:04."_ Without that the counter races itself — the matcher pages two clients at 11:04 and the receptionist offers the same slot on the phone at 11:06, and only one side knows.
- Tapping a gap is the walk-in entry point, pre-filled with that barber and that start.

**Ruling: a barber who is not rostered has no lane.** Not a hatched column, not "free day". Apple's schedule surfaces omit your colleague's day off; they do not draw it. Under the header: _"Off today: Niko, Stefan."_ A header toggle switches working/everyone, defaulting to working.

**Ruling: a barber who is deactivated but still has bookings on this day gets a lane.** Today they get none, because lanes come from `listActiveBarbers` — so a barber who leaves mid-month takes the rest of their booked clients off the sheet while `barberBusy` still holds the slots and the clients still walk in. The lane set is _rostered ∪ has-appointments-today_, and the departed barber's lane says so.

The five distinct nothings each need their own key in both catalogs: shop closed · not rostered (no lane) · rostered and genuinely open (the only case where "free day" is true, and it should read as **sellable capacity**, not emptiness) · a past day that had nothing · beyond the horizon.

### 4.5 The mixed party

Each seat draws in its own lane at its own start. The tie is minimal and deliberate:

- A small leading party glyph plus `2 of 3` in the trailing detail. That is the whole ruling.
- **Rejected:** accent connectors in the gutter and a cross-lane lift on touch. `uiInteractive` is the one sanctioned hover/press grammar and surfaces may not invent their own; a multi-element lift has no `:active` mirror, which the standing mobile-press-parity ruling requires; and on a phone the two lanes are never on screen together, so the mechanic pays only on a laptop and costs a bespoke gesture everywhere.
- **Rejected:** merging a party into one rectangle spanning columns. It claims Stefan's chair is occupied for the whole envelope when his seat may be forty minutes of a two-hour party, and it is structurally impossible when the arrangement is sequential.
- **Colour-per-party is banned.** An accent wash on a row already means `[data-selected]` — [list-row.ts:65](../../libs/ui/patterns/src/lib/list-row/list-row.ts:65) records the removal of a `tinted` rung for exactly that reason.

Sequential same-barber seats fuse into one continuous run with an internal hairline — `ui-list-group`'s own segmented idiom, not two boxes with a phantom gap.

### 4.6 Where the primary action lives

⚠️ **Superseded 2026-08-07:** the owner asked for an action that expands into the three things staff add — booking, block, waitlist. It lives **in `ui-page-action-bar`**, marked `uiPrimary` so it docks trailing in the thumb zone at every viewport — which means it inherits the house chrome fade (the quintic smootherstep ramp shared with the sheet chrome) and the safe-area floor rather than hand-rolling a second bottom edge on one page. The trigger is **`--sys-color-primary`, not accent**: accent is this surface's live signal — the now-line, today's column, the selected day, a booked block — and spending it again on permanent chrome puts the loudest colour on the one thing that never changes. It ships as a labelled vertical run rather than a fan of bare circles (a receptionist must not have to guess which circle blocks time), collapses on choosing one, and its motion is decoration the reduced-motion query removes entirely. Block time is live; booking and waitlist announce that they need the Phase B4 server path rather than silently doing nothing. Original ruling:

One prominent **Walk-in** in `ui-page-action-bar`, trailing edge, hugged, present on every day view. Nothing else on the page is prominent. The bar's documented contract is one primary trailing, intrinsic width, no full-width spread (owner ruling 2026-07-25) — so **day navigation does not go in it**; loading it with prev/today/next turns it into a toolbar.

### 4.7 States

| State                 | Treatment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Loading**           | Draw the day's _shape_ immediately — time gutter, lane headers, roster extent (barbers resolve synchronously from the catalog; windows arrive before appointments) — with `ui-skeleton` where visits will land. Today the first paint of a busy day is blank, and if the catalog resolves first every lane reads "Free day".                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Failed lane**       | A recessed panel in that lane with its own **Retry** that re-subscribes one listener, plus a warning dot on its header. Never destructive red — red already means cancel and no-show here, and spending it on a transient listener error teaches staff to discount it everywhere. The current copy ("reload the page") also throws away the per-lane isolation the store deliberately built, and lies: `subscribeWithRetry` is already retrying with backoff.                                                                             |
| **In flight**         | Spinner on **the pressed button only**; every other row in every other lane stays live. The pressed button keeps focus via `aria-disabled` + a click guard, never the native `disabled` attribute — which today drops focus to `<body>` mid-action ([accessibility-conventions.md](../accessibility-conventions.md) row 12 already specifies this and `UiButton` implements only half of it).                                                                                                                                             |
| **Refused**           | The message renders **in the row**, translated from `params['serverCode']`, and the row re-renders to its new truth: _"Already completed — Ivan, 14:12."_ No page banner. The listener supplies the new state within the same second anyway; the sentence names what happened so the loser of the race learns the model instead of concluding the button is broken.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Offline**           | Quiet indicator beside the date; lifecycle verbs go `aria-disabled` with an explanatory description. `persistentLocalCache` is enabled, so `onSnapshot` serves cached documents offline **and does not error** — the lane never trips `failed`, and a receptionist whose wifi died at 11:00 is looking at the 11:00 schedule at 15:00 with full confidence. Meanwhile `httpsCallable` does not queue: it hangs to the 70-second default and fails. Disabling the verbs costs nothing and prevents the exact lie the store refuses online. |
| **Empty (first run)** | Not a dead-end sentence. An admin sees _"Add your first barber"_ as an action; a barber sees _"Ask an admin to add you."_ Below it the day frame still draws — opening hours, chair count, current time — so a new receptionist can read the shop off the screen on their first shift.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

## 5. The verbs and their affordances

**Ruling: one clock-gated primary verb per row; everything else behind a trailing `uiMenuTrigger` ellipsis.** Today `OFFERED` renders every legal transition as a sibling 36pt button in a `flex-wrap` row — so a confirmed 18:00 booking at 10:00 offers Complete, No-show and Cancel with equal weight, three verbs that are all wrong, one of which is terminal. On a phone that is the exact geometry that produces the mis-tap. `--control-size-small` is `9 × 4px = 36px`, below the workspace's own 44 rung.

**Ruling: name the verbs what the shop does.** The button reads **Arrived** and **Done**; the badge keeps the lifecycle noun, because a badge describes a record and a button describes an act.

**⚠️ The Q2 ruling breaks the state that verb wrote to, and the repair is un-backfillable.** This brief originally read `pending` as _booked_ and `confirmed` as _this person is here_ — a reading available only because nothing auto-confirmed. Once an online booking lands `confirmed` at creation, `confirmed` can no longer carry arrival, and the front desk loses the signal entirely rather than gaining one.

**Ruling: add `arrivedAt` — a nullable instant, stamped by the Arrived verb, never a status transition.** ✅Verified absent from the entire workspace. The lifecycle union is untouched and `TRANSITIONS` keeps its shape; `Arrived` simply stops being an edge in the graph and becomes a stamp. The row's primary verb is then **Arrived** while `arrivedAt` is null from the start time onward, and **Done** after it. This is the same class of debt as Phase A's `bookedAt` — every visit served before the field lands has no punctuality record and never will — and it is the field that unblocks the measured "Waiting 6 min" over the fabricated countdown (§8), the front-desk queue (§8), and any lateness or punctuality figure on the scorecard (Phase D). **It ships with auto-confirm, in the same pass, or auto-confirm is a net loss of information.**

**Ruling on undo vs confirm, and why they split.** `TRANSITIONS` maps `completed`, `cancelled` and `no_show` all to `[]` — ✅verified at [appointment-status.ts:41](../../libs/domain/scheduling/src/lib/appointment-status.ts:41). A mis-tapped no-show permanently corrupts a client's record, a revenue figure and a retention metric with no in-product path back. **Add one bounded correction edge for `no_show → confirmed`**, actor-stamped and audited, as a compensating server write rather than a new edge in the graph. The domain already anticipated it: `CancellationReason` has a `no_show_converted` arm described as "a no-show the shop later reclassified", with zero references outside its own file.

Cancel does **not** get undo, and this is a real asymmetry rather than an oversight: cancelling frees a slot, `matchWaitlistOnBusyChange` fires immediately, and a five-second undo would have to either defer the match (slowing the waitlist for the common case) or page-then-retract. **Nothing external fires on a no-show, which is exactly why the correction edge goes there and the confirmation stays on cancel.**

**Ruling: the undo lives in the row's own action slot, not in a bottom toast.** A snackbar is foreign to a SwiftUI-parity system (SwiftUI has no such control; grep finds zero toast primitives in `libs/ui`), and on a forty-row page a bottom bar tells you _that_ you undid something at the opposite end of the screen from _which_.

**Ruling: the cancel sheet is a chip run over the closed union, and the note appears only under "Other".** ✅Verified: [transition-appointment.ts:128](../../apps/functions/src/lib/booking/transition-appointment.ts:128) stamps `{kind:'other', note: reason}` unconditionally, so **100% of staff cancellations ever recorded are `other`**. The union's own doc says the client/staff split "is the one that matters commercially — a shop that cancels its own bookings has a rostering problem, and a shop whose clients cancel has a reminder problem." `UiChip` exists. `TransitionAppointmentRequest.reason` widens to carry a code plus an optional note. This is roughly a day of work and it converts unrecoverable data loss into a working signal _while making the interaction faster_ — two taps instead of typing a sentence between cuts.

**Ruling on the cancel sheet's disclosure copy.** It must say what is true and only what is true. ✅Verified: **no appointment transition writes any notification** — `Notification.create` appears exactly once in `apps/functions`, in `match-waitlist.ts:169`. So _"Ivan will be told"_ would be a fabricated statement on the most destructive control on the surface. What is true and must be said: _"2 people are waiting for this slot and may be offered it automatically."_

**Ruling: the context menu can never be the only path.** Everything in the overflow appears in three synchronised places — the row's ellipsis, a long-press context menu, and the visit sheet — and the keyboard/screen-reader path is the ellipsis. On the web this is sharper than on iOS, because there is no VoiceOver rotor "Actions" fallback surfacing hidden row actions automatically.

**Provenance on the row.** `appendAudit` already records actor, action and instant on every transition. A settled row shows it: _"no-show · Maria, 14:12."_ In a shop where a receptionist and three barbers hold phones over the same forty rows, concurrency is the operating mode, and "who marked this complete?" is the question that decides whether staff trust the tool or start a paper backup. It is also what makes the correction edge safe: an undo that names its author is a fact; one that rewrites silently is worse than no undo. **This requires widening `auditLog`'s admin-only read to a staff read scoped to appointment `resourceId`s** — not opening the log wholesale.

## 8. What we deliberately do not build

This section is worth as much as §7.

**Drag-to-reschedule — rejected outright, at every phase.** Three independent lenses established that the lane is a list rather than a continuous surface; that `termsForBarber` means the same fade is 30 minutes with Ivan and 45 with Niko, so a block changes its own height when dragged sideways; that a party spans lanes, so dragging one seat either drags the envelope or violates the double-booking invariant; and that `reschedule` re-decides placement from scratch server-side and can refuse. That is a verdict, not a sequencing problem. "Ships second" is how a rejected feature survives review, so it is recorded here as rejected so it stops being re-proposed. The `/book` availability sheet is the whole feature; there is no second half.

**Swipe actions.** A genuinely new gesture primitive which, by its own accessibility requirement, must ship an ellipsis twin anyway — so it is pure additive cost over the menu, not an alternative. It collides with day paging, the writes are non-optimistic so a swipe that commits then bounces is exactly the lie the store was built to refuse, and this repo has already paid once for a custom drag-threshold saga (8px threshold, click suppression, disarm-on-next-pointerdown). Build the menu; stop.

**A three-column split view (nav | day | visit).** Column one duplicates navigation the shell owns, on a screen used by four people who know where they are — and the workspace explicitly collapsed four product scopes into one app for that reason. Column three is the visit sheet. What remains is a CSS grid sold as an architecture. HIG's "insert a content list between sidebar and detail" is guidance for a deep hierarchy; section → day → visit is a page with a detail sheet.

**iOS edit mode and multi-select bulk cancel.** The motivating event — a barber calls in sick with nine bookings — already has a _better_ primitive in the domain: write the `sick` `ScheduleException` and let `rebuildCapacityOnExceptionChange` cascade. Checkbox-selecting nine rows is the manual re-enactment of a modelled event. The right flow is: enter the absence, then the schedule offers _"Ivan is off — 9 visits need moving"_ as a worklist with the reason already coded to `staff_barber_absence` and #6's `BarberPref` flag sorting the list into "move silently" and "phone them". Multi-select also gives the row a fourth state and a leading slot it cannot spare.

**Shop-wide dead-time compression rails.** A solution to a problem the complete run does not have: once a gap is a row, a three-hour hole costs 44pt, not 400pt. It also contradicts itself — it argues staff distrust a schedule whose scale silently changes, then ships one whose scale changes on tap.

**~~A week grid, and a 3-day grid.~~ — OVERRULED 2026-08-07, both shipped.** The arithmetic below is still right, which is exactly why the multi-day views column by DATE and carry a barber picker rather than drawing thirty-five columns. Original reasoning:

**A week grid, and a 3-day grid.** Five chairs × seven days is thirty-five columns; to fit that you drop either the per-barber axis (then it is a heatmap) or the time axis (then it is a list with chrome). 3-day at five chairs is fifteen columns — the same arithmetic one step later. "How is next week looking" belongs on the insights surface as free-minutes decoration on the month calendar, read from the capacity rollup in one document.

**A persistent seven-day date-badge strip.** Always-on chrome for a question asked twice a day, duplicating the date sheet that is already two taps. ⚠️ **Built 2026-08-07 at the owner's request, then removed the same day** — with the calendar views shipped, the grid's own column headers already name the days (`пт 7`, the number in a capsule that fills on today, the reference's anatomy), so the strip was a second date row above the one the grid draws. The original reasoning survived contact.

**A month picker on the period label.** ⚠️ Not built, and now the surface's one gap: the strip was how a distant day was reached, and the chevrons only step ±1 period. `ui-calendar-scroller` in a sheet on the period label is the answer (feature #20) — the search reaches a known client, but not "show me next Saturday".

**Keyboard letter-verbs (C/D/N/X), `?` help, `/` search.** A single keypress that fires an irreversible no-show, on the screen with the highest agreed mis-tap risk. The roving-tabindex half survives; the vocabulary does not.

**A ticking `document.title` and Web Notifications for who's-next.** Both leak a client's name onto a shared counter device — the tab strip, the browser history, the OS shade. The research half-admitted this and proposed it anyway as "the weakest rung".

**A manual per-row "Remind" button.** Automatic T-24h reminders are the actual no-show reducer. A manual verb is an operator chore that invites double-nudging. And the effort is misstated everywhere: the "fully built notification infrastructure" is an **in-app inbox read only while the app is open** — `messagingSenderId` is literally `REPLACE_ME`, and the one live outbound channel is the OTP sender, which is permanently off-limits for non-login traffic. Reminders need a channel decision first.

**In-lane search, and any status filter.** Forty bookings is a scroll problem, not a search problem, and a schedule-local search teaches staff to look for people in the wrong place while only ever searching one day — client search is its own surface, and `findClients` is already built and indexed, contrary to §8 of the proposal. A status filter is actively harmful: hiding cancelled rows hides the reason a gap exists.

**Per-lane utilisation footers and any derived percentage on this surface.** The lane subscribes to appointments; a percentage would be wrong on any day with a break. The refusal is the ruling. Counts and next-start are facts the lane holds; percentages are the scorecard's job (Phase D).

**Money on the lane header.** Per-barber revenue needs a rules boundary that does not exist, and §9.1 says it must not be a template check.

**A countdown of "12 min left" derived from catalog duration.** A projection presented as fact, on a screen graded on not fabricating anything — and the whole reason to want `arrivedAt` is that catalog durations are fiction. Keep the _measured_ form: "Waiting 6 min".

**DST-day offset labels and a struck-through missing hour.** Sofia's transitions happen between 03:00 and 04:00; the shop is asleep. Two ambiguous rows that can never contain an appointment, twice a year, in exchange for a branch in the time formatter.

**A durable guest-note store.** Correct diagnosis, wrong surface: a new collection, new rules, a retention policy, and a durable free-text record of opinions about named people belongs to the clients surface with its own review. The only part that lands here is a copy constraint — the visit sheet must not invite staff to write durable facts into a booking note.

**A front-desk kanban board.** Worth nothing until an arrival timestamp exists; with today's five statuses it would have one column holding the entire day and three empty ones.

**Promoting the time-grid layout into `libs/ui`.** One screen's layout concern with one consumer. "Reuse, never copy" means finding the original before duplicating, not pre-generalising. Promote when a second surface asks.

## 9. Accessibility, i18n and resilience

**The honest answer on screen-reader access to a time grid: there is no time grid.** The proportional layout is a CSS-only encoding — `grid-row` from a computed minute track on `li` elements whose DOM order is start-ordered per lane. There is no `role="grid"`, because APG scopes that pattern to tabular data and composite widgets and it has no way to express spans of unequal length or overlaps; and there is no `aria-hidden` parallel list, because it would drift the first time someone edited one and not the other. **A screen-reader user hears "list, 7 items, item 3 of 7" — the same list a sighted user sees, positioned differently.** A 3–5 chair shop has at most ~40 rows a day; a list is the honest shape, not a degradation. Apple Calendar and Google Calendar both do exactly this.

**Every row needs one composed accessible name.** Today a row is a stack of unlabelled spans, `timeLabel` uses a U+2013 en dash that reads as "09:00 09:30", and the action buttons carry only the verb — so a barber tabbing the page hears "Confirm, Cancel, Complete, No-show, Cancel…" with no idea which visit any of them belongs to. That is the highest-risk path to a wrong-client cancellation on the surface. Compose one visually-hidden sentence per `li`, reference it from every action button via `aria-labelledby`, and `aria-hidden` the visual fragments. The technique already exists in the repo at `booking-schedule-step.html:108`.

**One polite live region, deliberately sparse.** Speak only for things the user caused or must act on: the new date after a chevron, the outcome of a transition, a lane going stale or recovering. Announce nothing for Firestore-driven changes to rows the user is not on — a busy Saturday would narrate a stranger's booking every few seconds. One exception: a change landing on the focused row, because the buttons under their finger just changed meaning. **Remove the three current `role="alert"` nodes**: two of them render the same message simultaneously, and the lane-failure alert re-fires on every retry flap.

**Status in three channels.** Word (already correct — the badge carries `appointments.status.*`), shape (a leading glyph — `ui-badge` already ships `gap: var(--sys-space-tight)` and default `<ng-content/>`, so this is a **call-site change with zero component work**), colour third. Colour must be third because ⚠️ **every badge tone fails AA on the surface it actually renders on** — measured against `--sys-color-surface-secondary` (#f7f7f7, the list-group segment fill): accent 2.45:1, warning 3.34:1, success 3.41:1, destructive 4.22:1. Fix: foreground ink on the tone tint, tone colour moves to the glyph. Separately and urgently: `borderedProminent` + `destructive` in dark theme is `--sys-color-on-primary` (#0d0d0d) on #d32f2f = **3.90:1**, on the "Cancel the appointment" button — the most irreversible control in the product, in the theme a barber's phone is most likely to be in. Swapping to `--sys-color-on-accent` gives 4.98:1. One line. `badge.css` already learned this lesson one file over and the button never got the memo.

**Also fix while here:** `.staff-visit__note` hardcodes `color: var(--sys-color-foreground)` and therefore beats the `[data-terminal]` recess — so a cancelled visit's client note is the loudest thing on a dead row. And **completed stays at full ink**: it is the day's achievement. Only cancelled stands down. The current CSS greys all three terminal states identically, rendering three opposite meanings as one.

**Dynamic Type and 200% zoom.** Spacing is rem-based (`--sys-space-unit: calc(0.25rem × var(--sys-density))`) so the layout scales with browser font size — that part is right. What is missing: `em`-based **container** queries on the row and the lane header, so the row re-stacks when the text grows rather than when the viewport shrinks. A 1280px laptop at 200% text is, in Apple's terms, horizontally constrained, and today it gets the desktop layout with crowding. **Below the width the proportional tier can honour, the time axis is the first thing dropped, not the last** — the run degrades to the phone rendering. Nothing truncates: a client's name and their number are exactly what someone enlarged the text to read.

**Reduced transparency has no handler in the live system.** It is implemented in exactly one file workspace-wide — a library with zero consumers. The live material recipe at `libs/ui/modifiers/modifiers.css` has no such block, so the cancel sheet's chrome sits on a 78%-translucent blurred fill with schedule rows showing through the confirm button. Add the block beside the existing reduced-motion one. (Reduced _motion_ is genuinely well handled — 18 components carry the query — and needs nothing.)

**i18n.** Both catalogs are at exact parity (672/672) and `staff.*` is 18 keys with real Bulgarian copy — the one staff surface already meeting the §9.6 bar. This design roughly quadruples that subtree in two languages, and the five zero-states, the six cancellation reasons, the eight exception kinds and the gap-row copy all need keys before anything ships. Two live defects to fix in the same pass: `CatalogPresenter.locale` is a bare language tag, so `formatMoney(money, 'en')` returns **"€28.00"** against the standing "28,00 €" ruling — derive a separate region-qualified formatting locale pinned to the shop, because currency, dates and week start follow where the shop _is_, not what language the reader prefers. And `errors.booking.transition.not_allowed` interpolates raw enum values, rendering «от „confirmed“ към „no_show“» inside Bulgarian copy. Week start is hardcoded Monday; `Intl.Locale.getWeekInfo` is unavailable in this runtime.

Worth stating against a common assumption: **Bulgarian is not systematically longer** — the staff verbs are near-identical ("Complete" 8 → "Приключи" 8). The real Cyrillic risk is different: "Не дойде" contains a space and will break mid-verb in a narrow wrapping action row, and Cyrillic sets wider per character in Latin-designed faces.

**DST.** ✅Verified correct in the day walk (§3). The domain is rigorous — `CalendarDay` documents that a day is 23 or 25 hours and the engine enumerates both occurrences of the ambiguous hour. The presentation gap (two rows both reading 03:30) is real and deliberately not fixed (§8).

**Offline.** Detailed in §4.7. The one-line rule: **a stale schedule must never be indistinguishable from a live one**, and the pen is disabled while it is stale — because the callable will fail anyway, and preventing the write prevents exactly the lie the store refuses when online.

**Testing gate.** The existing component spec stubs five ports and asserts three things, all about the date header, because its catalog stub returns no barbers — so not one lane, row, transition or sheet on this surface is under test today. Any new surface adds `AVAILABILITY_READER` and `WAITLIST_READER` to that stub list before it can assert anything. **This is a phase gate, not a nice-to-have.**

**Measurement.** No baselines exist anywhere in the research, and "the most frequent action is the cheapest one" is unfalsifiable without frequencies. Before Phase B2, half a day against the shop's real Firestore data: walk-ins/day, cancellations/week, phone vs online split, and rows scrolled past on a Saturday. It would settle more open disagreements than another research lens.

## 11. Open questions needing an owner ruling

1. **Should a walk-in be permitted before `minLeadMinutes` and outside a roster window?** ✅Verified contradiction: `decideBooking` refuses both unconditionally, while owner ruling R1 says staff placement is unbounded and `OccupancyBlock.outsideWindow` exists to count it as overtime. The policy default is `minLeadMinutes: 120`, and its own comment claims it "keeps same-day walk-ups bookable". _Recommendation: one `staffPlacement` flag on the callable, role-gated, relaxing both — resolved as a single "is the caller staff" decision alongside Q3, or the two will drift again._ ⚠️ **Q3 was granted on 2026-08-07 and this is now the unanswered half of that same decision.** The staff branch on `reschedule` and the staff branch on `commitBooking` should be one server-side notion of staff authority written once; ruling them apart is exactly how they drift.
2. ✅ **RULED 2026-08-07 — yes, online bookings auto-confirm.** `Appointment.create` stamps `pending`, the client's screen reads "Requested", and `BookingPolicy` had no `autoConfirm` field — so the manual gate was never a shop decision, it was an accident of the model. **Add the field, default it on, keep manual confirmation as an opt-in.** Consequences, all now settled: the pending inbox (#26) is dropped; "Confirm" disappears from the most common row; the client stops seeing "Requested" for a booking the shop was always going to take. **The one consequence that is not free: `confirmed` can no longer mean "this person is here", so `arrivedAt` ships in the same pass (§5) and moves into B0 because it cannot be backfilled.**
3. ✅ **RULED 2026-08-07 — yes, staff may move a client's booking, including inside the cancellation window, audited.** ✅Verified: `reschedule` refuses any caller who is not `ownerUserId` and applies `mayCancelAt`, while `transitionAppointment` documents the opposite doctrine ("the shop itself may always move its own book"). The ruling resolves that contradiction in favour of `transitionAppointment`'s doctrine. **⚠️ Half of this question survives the ruling and still needs an answer: a guest-only walk-in has `ownerUserId: null` and is therefore unmovable by anyone, including its creator — a staff branch keyed on "is the caller staff" fixes the staff path but leaves the guest record ownerless.** _Recommendation: decide who owns a guest-only appointment before the walk-in button ships (Q1/B4), because it cannot be backfilled._
4. **Does a receptionist see absence _reasons_?** `sick` is Art. 9 data; the public projection is structurally reason-free by design. Recording the reason at all needs a staff-only sibling collection with its own rules pair. _Recommendation: a receptionist writes and reads "unavailable"; only a manager sees the kind. The split lives in the control, not in a template check._
5. **Is the barber's real surface this screen at all?** A barber needs one line — who is next and when. Filtering a five-lane day down to one is more expensive to build and get right on a phone than a single-row surface would be, and it is blocked anyway on a `Barber ↔ User` link that was deliberately removed. _Recommendation: the counter is the real user of `/staff/schedule`. Build the chair's answer as small as possible — the NOW/NEXT card is already most of it — rather than as a filtered day._
6. **What is the outbound channel for anything the shop tells a client?** ✅Verified: no appointment transition writes a notification of any kind, the in-app inbox reaches only clients with the app open, and the only live sender is the OTP identity, which the standing ruling keeps separate from campaigns. This gates reminders, confirmation-tells-the-client, and any honest disclosure copy on the cancel sheet. _Recommendation: decide the campaign channel (Viber/SMS identity, or Web Push on the service worker that already ships) before Phase B4, and until then say only what is true._
7. **Does the shop want a walk-in queue as its own object?** Every product in this category treats a walk-in as a degenerate appointment; the walk-in-native tools treat the queue as the primary object. A Bulgarian 3–5 chair shop is substantially a walk-in business, and `SeatLabel`, `BookingOrigin: 'walk_in'` and the live capacity projection are most of a queue already. _Recommendation: not on this surface, and not this phase — but it is the one place this product could be genuinely better than the incumbents rather than merely adequate, and it deserves its own brief._
